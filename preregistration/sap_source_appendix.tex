\subsection{Statistical Analysis: Extended Justification}
\label{app:sap-extended}

\subsubsection{The outcome pair and its hierarchy: primary accuracy, key-secondary success time}
\label{app:coprimary-rationale}
The trial records two outcomes because the theory defines the construct as two-dimensional. CLT decomposes skill acquisition into schema \emph{construction}---whether a correct response can be produced at all---and schema \emph{automation}---the fluency with which it is produced \parencite{sweller1998,kalyuga2003}. Accuracy indexes construction; success time indexes automation. The classical skill-acquisition literature makes the same decomposition, defining acquisition as joint improvement in accuracy \emph{and} speed \parencite{proctor1995,fitts1967}. Measuring both therefore operationalises the whole construct rather than truncating it to either dimension. The two measures are also mutually constraining: because speed and accuracy trade off against one another \parencite{heitz2014}, neither is interpretable in isolation---an arm may appear accurate only by responding slowly and cautiously, or fast only by guessing. Anchoring time on \emph{correct} responses collapses the two dimensions into an index of correct-response efficiency that fast guessing cannot inflate \parencite{vandierendonck2017}, neutralising this strategic confound. The pair is finally complementary along the Bloom gradient: lower-order items risk accuracy ceilings, where the compressed accuracy distribution loses discriminating power while success time remains continuous; on higher-order items, where few succeed and times are heavily censored, accuracy carries the signal.

Measuring both, however, does not require confirming on both. The confirmatory decision is carried by accuracy alone, with success time designated a key secondary outcome, for three reasons. First, a single pre-specified primary variable is the standard for a confirmatory trial \parencite{ich1998}; % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [ich1998] sec 2.2.2, p.7: "There should generally be only one primary variable." | read directly from the EMA Step-5 PDF (CPMP/ICH/363/96), ema.europa.eu.
multiple primary endpoints require an explicit rule for what counts as trial success and an inferential architecture to match \parencite{pocock1997}. Second, construction is theoretically prior to automation: a schema that was never constructed cannot be automated, so the sharpest and least model-dependent prediction concerns accuracy, and the automation prediction presupposes the construction one. Third, the alternatives are strictly worse at this sample size. Co-primary endpoints in the regulatory sense (both must succeed) control the type~I rate without adjustment but compound the type~II rate---the trial fails unless two effects are detected simultaneously---a material cost at 66 per arm. Splitting $\alpha$ across an either-wins pair (the 0.025/0.025 convention) halves the level of every test while licensing only the weak disjunctive claim that \emph{something} differed. A composite of accuracy and time would have no substantive scale. The hierarchy preserves the theoretical pairing---both outcomes are estimated, reported, and interpreted together---while giving the confirmatory claim a single unambiguous target.

\subsubsection{Primary accuracy model: estimand anchoring, model choice, covariates, and diagnostics}
\label{app:accuracy-model}
The mixed-model formulation of Equation~\eqref{eq:primary-acc} is chosen over the two natural alternatives. Against \emph{separate per-wave analyses} (one ANCOVA-type model per wave): the single fit expresses the immediate-wave contrasts and their delayed-wave change as parameters of one model, so the persistence hypothesis (H5$_a$) is a within-model linear combination ($\beta_2+\beta_5$) rather than a comparison across separately fitted models, and participants who provide only the immediate wave still contribute to all fixed effects through the likelihood \parencite{detry2016,bell2020}. % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [bell2020] Background (exact): "its unbiasedness for data missing completely at random or at random" -- CORRECTION: the earlier "(MCAR)"/"which are" were NOT in the source. | Bell & Rabe, Trials 2020;21:148, PMC7006144. (Paper is framed for cluster RCTs; the MCAR/MAR unbiasedness property it states is general.)
With $\mathrm{Wave}$ dummy-coded (immediate $=0$), the arm main effects are anchored at the immediate assessment and so coincide with the primary estimand, the pre-specified target of estimation \parencite{ich2019}; the per-wave \textcite{lin2013} robustness estimator (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}) retains a fully assumption-light single-wave check. Against \emph{repeated-measures ANOVA}: RM-ANOVA discards any participant missing a wave (unbiased only under the far stronger missing-completely-at-random assumption), accommodates continuous prognostic covariates awkwardly, and yields omnibus wave-by-arm $F$-tests rather than the directional, wave-anchored contrasts the hypotheses specify. The mixed model for repeated measures is the standard confirmatory choice in trials with post-randomisation repeated outcomes precisely because it uses all available data and remains valid under missing-at-random given the modelled variables \parencite{detry2016,bell2020}.

With two waves, the participant random intercept is equivalent to a compound-symmetry covariance with a common residual variance. This is a restriction, not merely a convenience, and two pre-specified guards would surface any distortion from wave-specific variances: the per-wave \textcite{lin2013} estimator (which imposes no cross-wave structure at all) and the item-level crossed-random-effects model (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}, item~4). An unstructured covariance---the conventional MMRM default \parencite{mallinckrodt2008}, which with only two waves adds a single wave-specific variance parameter---is pre-specified as a sensitivity fit and reported in place of the compound-symmetric model if a likelihood-ratio test rejects the equal-variance restriction; because the primary immediate-wave contrasts are point-identified independently of the cross-wave covariance, the covariance structure bears only on the precision of the persistence estimand. Attrition concentrates at the delayed wave, so the primary immediate-wave contrasts are essentially complete-data by design; the missing-at-random assumption bears on the persistence estimand and is probed by the multiple-imputation and Lee-bounds sensitivity analyses (Missing data and attrition).

re-analyses of completed trials show substantial realised power gains from prognostic adjustment \parencite{kahan2014}, % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [kahan2014] abstract: "Adjustment for known prognostic covariates led to large increases in power for most outcomes." conclusions: "Adjustment for known prognostic covariates can lead to substantial increases in power, and should be routinely incorporated into the analysis of randomized trials." | Trials 2014;15:139, PMC4022337 (open access), full text.
and fitting baseline measurements as covariates is the standard recommendation for trials of this structure \parencite{vickers2001} % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [vickers2001] p.1123: "An additional advantage of analysis of covariance is that it generally has greater statistical power to detect a treatment effect than the other methods." no significance tests of baseline balance are conducted. \emph{Assumption diagnostics.} Residual and random-intercept normality are inspected with quantile--quantile plots. The bounded outcome (a proportion over 16 items) is guarded by the fractional-logit robustness model \parencite{papke1996} and by the item-level logistic specification, both of which respect the outcome's support; a ceiling-compressed accuracy distribution would surface there. The linear specification is nonetheless retained as the primary model on three grounds: it targets the marginal mean difference in percentage points---the directly interpretable, pre-registered effect scale; the ordinary-least-squares estimator remains consistent for that average treatment effect under randomisation despite the bounded, non-normal outcome \parencite{angrist2009}; and at the assumed control mean of $0.60$ over sixteen items the distribution sits well away from either bound, where the Gaussian approximation is close. A pre-registered simulation of this exact estimator on binomial-granularity outcomes returned nominal Type~I error, and the fractional-logit and item-level logistic fits are reported alongside the linear model wherever they disagree. Wald inference from the REML fit relies on the large-sample normal approximation, defensible at $N = 200$; the pre-registered randomisation inference (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}, item~5) supplies a backstop whose validity derives from the physical act of randomisation alone \parencite{young2019}.

\subsubsection{Key-secondary success-time model: estimand, competing risks, clustering, and diagnostics}
\label{app:successtime-model}
\emph{Estimand.} The event is the submission of a correct answer; incorrect, ``I do not know'', and unanswered items are censored at their recorded response time. The arm coefficients are therefore \emph{cause-specific} log hazard ratios for correct production: the instantaneous rate at which a still-unresolved item is converted into a correct answer. This is the survival-analysis expression of fluency, and it is the appropriate estimand here because the competing destination---an incorrect submission---is not a nuisance process to be marginalised over but the very phenomenon the primary endpoint measures. The competing-risks alternative, the Fine--Gray subdistribution hazard \parencite{austin2016}, % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [austin2016] title confirmed: "Introduction to the Analysis of Survival Data in the Presence of Competing Risks" | Circulation 2016;133:601-609.
targets the cumulative incidence of correct answers and therefore re-imports accuracy into the time endpoint, duplicating Equation~\eqref{eq:primary-acc} in a less interpretable metric; it is not used. The naive alternative---comparing mean response times among correctly answered items only---conditions on a post-treatment variable and is biased when correctness itself responds to treatment \parencite{montgomery2018post}; the participant-level median time-to-correct and log rate correct score (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}, item~7) are retained only as transparency checks that do not replace the censored formulation.

\emph{Inference and diagnostics.} Because items are nested in participants, inference uses the robust sandwich covariance estimator clustered at the participant level, which remains valid under within-cluster dependence and model misspecification \parencite{lin1989}. % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [lin1989] abstract: "under a possibly misspecified Cox proportional hazards model" (title: "The Robust Inference for the Cox Proportional Hazards Model") | JASA 1989;84(408):1074-1078.
Ties are handled with the Efron approximation. The proportional-hazards assumption is assessed with scaled Schoenfeld residuals and the associated score test \parencite{grambsch1994}; % VERIFY-PENDING 2026-07-12 [grambsch1994]: title + venue confirmed ("Proportional Hazards Tests and Diagnostics Based on Weighted Residuals", Biometrika 1994;81(3):515-526) and it is the standard PH score-test/diagnostic reference; exact phrase "time-weighted score tests..." NOT re-confirmed verbatim from the retrieved abstract -- verify against full text.
if it is violated for the arm indicators, the pre-specified fallback is a log-normal accelerated failure time model reporting time ratios, with the Cox estimates retained as descriptive. Response time is measured from stimulus onset to answer submission; no administrative cap is applied, following the pre-specified rationale that extreme values are handled by the speeding-exclusion sensitivity check (item~8 below) rather than by censoring. One caveat is stated rather than hidden: censoring an item at the moment of an incorrect submission is informative if fast-but-wrong responders differ systematically from slow responders. The cause-specific estimand remains well defined under this regime, and the scoring-sensitivity and speeding-exclusion analyses (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}, items~6 and~8) bound the influence of fast incorrect responding on the conclusions.

\subsubsection{Multiplicity control, gatekeeping, and the non-inferiority test}
\label{app:multiplicity}
\emph{Why a fixed sequence.} The confirmatory hypotheses are ordered a priori by theoretical priority: H1$_a$ (outsourcing impairs), then H2$_a$ (scaffolding is not harmful), then the Socratic--Unrestricted supporting contrast. Testing each at level $\alpha$ and stopping at the first non-rejection controls the familywise error rate in the strong sense: a type~I error can occur only at the first true null hypothesis in the sequence, and that hypothesis is only reached through rejections of the hypotheses ordered before it, each tested at level $\alpha$ \parencite{bauer1991,maurer1995,westfall2001,dmitrienko2013,fda2017}. The procedure spends no multiplicity tax on the primary contrast, whereas a flat Holm correction across the three contrasts \parencite{holm1979} would control the same error rate while testing the primary contrast at $\alpha/3$ in the worst case---buying protection the a-priori ordering already provides, at the price of power on the one contrast the trial is designed around. The structural cost of the sequence is accepted by design: if H1$_a$ is not rejected, H2$_a$ is never formally tested and the non-inferiority claim is forfeit even if true. This is theoretically coherent: the scaffolding question---does withholding the answer protect learning?---presupposes that unscaffolded answer provision harms it; absent that harm, H2$_a$ loses its comparator logic. The graphical $\alpha$-recycling alternative \parencite{bretz2009}, % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [bretz2009] title confirmed: "A Graphical Approach to Sequentially Rejective Multiple Test Procedures" | Stat Med 2009;28(4):586-604.
which would allow H2$_a$ to be tested after an H1$_a$ failure by splitting $\alpha$ across branches, was considered and rejected because it taxes the primary contrast, and protecting the primary at full $\alpha$ is the design priority. The ordering is fixed before data collection and is never reordered on observed $p$-values, a requirement for the validity of the procedure.

\emph{The non-inferiority test.} H2$_a$ asserts that Socratic scaffolding is not harmful relative to search within a tolerated margin: $H_0\colon \beta_1 \le -\Delta$ against $H_1\colon \beta_1 > -\Delta$. This is the operative one-sided test of the two one-sided tests (TOST) procedure \parencite{schuirmann1987,lakens2017,lakens2018}; % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [schuirmann1987] title confirmed: "A Comparison of the Two One-Sided Tests Procedure and the Power Approach for Assessing the Equivalence of Average Bioavailability" | J Pharmacokinet Biopharm 1987;15(6):657-680.
operationally, non-inferiority is declared if and only if the lower limit of the two-sided 90\% confidence interval for $\beta_1$ lies above $-\Delta$, and the interval is reported whatever the verdict so that an underpowered result remains informative as an estimate \parencite{cumming2014,amrhein2019}. The dedicated test exists because its naive substitute is invalid: a non-significant superiority test cannot establish absence of harm---absence of evidence is not evidence of absence \parencite{altmanbland1995}---whereas the margin test can positively reject harm larger than $\Delta$.

\emph{The margin.} A non-inferiority margin must be pre-specified and justified on substantive grounds, never widened to make power feasible \parencite{piaggio2012,ema2005,althunian2017}. The margin here follows the preservation-of-effect logic \parencite{althunian2017,dagostino2003}: the reference effect is the deficit the trial is designed to detect, the $\approx$17-percentage-point Control--Unrestricted difference anchoring H1 \parencite{shen2026}, and the margin requires the Socratic arm to preserve at least 50\% of the control's advantage over the harmful comparator, giving $\Delta = 0.5 \times 17 \approx 8.5$ percentage points. The margin thus sits well below the superiority anchor (coherence between the two claims), and the non-inferiority comparison is separately powered against it---approximately $0.87$ at a true Socratic--Google difference of zero---in Appendix~\ref{app:power}. % D6-locked; NI power = the D10-corrected 0.87 (one-sided alpha=.05 / 90%-CI rule; the earlier 0.79 priced a stricter 95%-CI rule) -- matches app:power. A stricter one-item margin (6.25 pp) remains available at a documented power cost (~0.66); see CLAUDE.md D6/D10.

\emph{Analysis population for the non-inferiority claim.} Intention-to-treat is not conservative for non-inferiority: noncompliance and cross-arm contamination blur differences between arms and therefore push toward spurious equivalence. Following the CONSORT extension for non-inferiority trials \parencite{piaggio2012}, the H2$_a$ conclusion requires consistency between the intent-to-treat and per-protocol estimates (Compliance, contamination, and complier effects), with any discrepancy reported and interpreted against the logged compliance data.

\emph{Secondary and lower families.} The two secondary confirmatory accuracy hypotheses---the Bloom-order interaction (H4$_a$) and delayed-wave persistence (H5$_a$)---form a Holm family at familywise $\alpha = 0.05$, one-sided \parencite{holm1979}. The key-secondary success-time contrasts form a separate Holm family interpreted as supporting evidence, never as substitutes for the primary conclusion. The aided-block productivity indicators are controlled at a false discovery rate \parencite{benjamini1995} % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [benjamini1995] title confirmed: "Controlling the False Discovery Rate: A Practical and Powerful Approach to Multiple Testing" | JRSS-B 1995;57(1):289-300.
as converging indicators. Exploratory quantities (H4$_b$, H5$_b$, the four-level Bloom trend, moderation analyses) are reported as estimates with confidence intervals and carry no error-rate claim \parencite{frumento2025}.

\subsubsection{Secondary analysis I: Bloom-level concentration (full specification)}
\label{app:bloom-analysis}
The four administered Bloom levels are treated as an \emph{ordered} gradient of element interactivity, Remember $<$ Understand $<$ Analyze $<$ Evaluate, whose ordering---but not spacing---is licensed by the taxonomy and corroborated empirically \parencite{anderson2001,krathwohl2002,srinivas2025}.

The binary lower- versus higher-order partition of Equation~\eqref{eq:bloom} is preferred to a finer level-by-level analysis on three grounds. First, the lower- versus higher-order cut is the theoretically load-bearing distinction and is itself uncontested \parencite{anderson2001}, whereas whether each adjacent level is strictly more demanding than the last is disputed \parencite{hamamotofilho2020,ravand2025}; the contrast rests only on the former. Second, with only four items per level per wave, an eight-versus-eight contrast is materially better powered than a four-point trend. Third, the closest published precedent---a four-arm comparison of GenAI, web search, an e-textbook, and a no-tool control across Bloom-stratified tasks---recovered this pattern with a coarse per-level analysis: the tool advantage was pronounced for lower-order and application items and absent at the highest, analysis-and-evaluation level, a \emph{step} at the lower- to higher-order boundary rather than a smooth gradient \parencite{akgun2025}. Throughout, whether the deficit \emph{differs} across order levels is adjudicated by the interaction term itself, never by contrasting a significant effect at one level against a non-significant effect at another---an inference that is not valid \parencite{nieuwenhuis2011,gelman2006}. Because interactions on the log-odds scale need not correspond to interactions on the probability scale \parencite{ainorton2003}, % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [ainorton2003] title confirmed: "Interaction Terms in Logit and Probit Models" | Econ Letters 2003;80(1):123-129.
the focal arm $\times$ order contrast is additionally reported as an average marginal interaction contrast on the probability scale. The confirmatory interaction is estimated by fitting the mean structure of Equation~\eqref{eq:bloom} as a participant-clustered logistic GEE (exchangeable working correlation), whose one-sided test is well calibrated in simulation; the crossed-random-effects Bayesian GLMM is reported alongside as corroboration only, because its variational posterior standard deviations are anticonservative and would overstate the interaction's precision.

As a pre-specified but supportive refinement, the full four-level ordering is examined with the arm $\times$ linear-polynomial interaction and a Jonckheere--Terpstra test against the monotone alternative, which assumes ordering without spacing \parencite{jonckheere1954}. Because interaction tests are markedly less efficient than tests of main effects \parencite{mcclelland1993} and the per-level cells are thin, this trend is reported with point estimates and confidence intervals emphasised over significance verdicts \parencite{frumento2025} and treated as corroboration of, not a substitute for, the binary result.

Two analyses are exploratory. First, whether the higher-order concentration appears at acquisition, at retention, or both is examined by adding the wave factor and the arm $\times$ order $\times$ wave interaction to Equation~\eqref{eq:bloom}, the item-level analogue of the persistence analysis; the precedent above found tool advantages that were positive immediately yet eroded by a delayed test \parencite{akgun2025}, so the deficit may surface at the delayed wave rather than the immediate one. As three-way interactions on four-item cells are severely underpowered, this is read from estimates and intervals alone. Second, should lower-order \emph{sparing} be claimed, the Unrestricted--Search difference among the lower-order items is assessed for equivalence against the pre-registered equivalence bound of 6.25 percentage points (one Mini-VLAT item) in each wave \parencite{lakens2017,lakens2018}---an equivalence threshold distinct from the non-inferiority preservation margin $\Delta$ (Appendix~\ref{app:multiplicity})---so that a claim of protection rests on a positive equivalence test rather than a non-significant difference. % [SESOI-LO] LOCKED = 6.25 pp, resolved 2026-07-14 (CLAUDE.md D9).
The success-time analogue of the moderation hypothesis (H4$_b$) is likewise exploratory: order-specific hazard ratios are reported with confidence intervals, without significance verdicts. The assumed level ordering is validated within each wave by confirming that mean item difficulty rises across the four levels, which doubles as a parallel-form check.

\subsubsection{Secondary analysis II: productivity (frontier plot and affordance check)}
\label{app:productivity}
The rate correct score is analysed on the log scale because its sampling distribution is strongly right-skewed, and the aided productivity outcomes of Equation~\eqref{eq:productivity} are controlled at a false discovery rate \parencite{benjamini1995} rather than individually because they function as converging indicators rather than confirmatory claims. The productivity--learning trade-off itself is summarised descriptively as an arm-level frontier plot of aided throughput against the immediate-wave unassisted accuracy of Equation~\eqref{eq:primary-acc}; the confirmatory weight of the trade-off claim rests on the primary accuracy endpoint, not on this plot. These analyses simultaneously serve as the affordance manipulation check: a markedly higher aided throughput and aided success-hazard in the Unrestricted arm confirms that the arms differed in solution-provision capability.

\subsubsection{Robustness analyses}
\label{app:robustness}
The primary and key-secondary conclusions should be robust to the following pre-specified perturbations.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unadjusted estimates.} Difference-in-means (accuracy) and an unadjusted Cox model (success time), demonstrating independence from the covariate model.
    \item \textbf{Fully interacted adjustment.} The \textcite{lin2013} estimator applied to each wave's accuracy separately: treatment indicators, mean-centred covariates, and all treatment--covariate interactions with sandwich standard errors. % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [lin2013] abstract: "OLS adjustment cannot hurt asymptotic precision when a full set of treatment-covariate interactions is included." | Ann Appl Stat 2013;7(1):295-318 (also arXiv:1208.2301).
    \item \textbf{Bounded-outcome specification.} A fractional logit model per wave estimated by quasi-maximum likelihood \parencite{papke1996}, % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [papke1996] abstract: "simple quasi-likelihood estimation methods for regression models with a fractional dependent variable" | J Appl Econometrics 1996;11(6):619-632.
    reporting average marginal effects, guarding against ceiling-effect distortion in the linear model.
    \item \textbf{Item-level accuracy model.} A logistic mixed-effects model on item-level correctness with crossed random intercepts for participants and items \parencite{baayen2008}, % [baayen2008]: quoted at first citation (Bloom-level analysis).
    with arm and wave as fixed effects, verifying that participant-level aggregation masks no item heterogeneity.
    \item \textbf{Randomisation inference.} Permutation $p$-values for the primary contrast in both the accuracy and success-time models, based on 10{,}000 re-randomisations of arm labels with the full estimation pipeline re-run per draw, following evidence that conventional asymptotic tests can overstate significance in experimental samples of this size \parencite{young2019}. % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [young2019] abstract: "using randomization statistical inference to test the null hypothesis of no treatment effects" | QJE 2019;134(2):557-598.
    \item \textbf{Scoring sensitivity.} Accuracy re-estimated with ``I do not know'' responses (a) scored as incorrect, the primary coding, and (b) excluded so accuracy is computed over attempted items. The success-time endpoint is structurally invariant to this choice, since ``I do not know'' responses are censored under either convention.
    \item \textbf{Simple success-time summaries.} Participant-level median time-to-correct and the log rate correct score on the unassisted blocks, analysed in the linear framework, as transparency checks on the Cox result.
    \item \textbf{Data-quality exclusions.} Re-estimation excluding participants flagged by the pre-registered speeding rule (median item response time below 1{,}000\,ms) or failing the embedded attention check, applied symmetrically across arms. This rule also limits the influence of inattentive responding on all response-time outcomes.
\end{enumerate}
