\subsection{Statistical Analysis Plan}
\label{sec:sap}

\paragraph{Identification strategy.}
The primary estimand is specified with intent-to-treat \parencite{ich1998}, analysing the effect of being \emph{assigned} to a tool condition, irrespective of how intensively or faithfully the tool was used. All randomised participants who provide outcome data are analysed in the arm to which they were randomised,
as required for the confirmatory analysis of a parallel-group trial under CONSORT \parencite{hopewell2025}. % [hopewell2025]: already in the thesis bibliography; no quote needed.
Intercurrent events, namely use of external tools during training (relevant for the search arm) and post test (disclosed via the end-of-study item) and 
minimal engagement with the assigned tool, are handled under the 
treatment-policy strategy in the primary analysis; 
complier-specific estimands are reported as supplementary analyses (see Compliance below). 
One qualification applies as H2$_a$ is a non-inferiority claim, for which intention-to-treat is not conservative, we therefore also discuss the per-protocol analysis as recommended by the CONSORT extension for non-inferiority trials \parencite{piaggio2012} (see Hypothesis testing and Compliance).

\paragraph{Measurement and outcome hierarchy.}
Two outcomes are measured on every block (training and both unaided post-tests), with a pre-specified hierarchy between them:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Accuracy} (\emph{primary outcome}): the proportion of correct responses on each 16-item unassisted block;
    \item \textbf{Success time} (\emph{key secondary outcome}): the per-item time elapsed from item onset to the submission of a \emph{correct} answer. Items answered incorrectly, answered with ``I do not know'', or left unanswered are treated as right-censored at their recorded response time, so that every administered item contributes information.
\end{enumerate}
The censoring formulation is essential: restricting a response-time analysis to correctly answered items would condition on a post-treatment variable, since correctness is itself affected by the manipulation, and such conditioning can distort treatment-effect estimates \parencite{montgomery2018post}. % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [montgomery2018post] abstract: "scholars often unwittingly distort treatment effect estimates by conditioning on variables that could be affected by their experimental manipulation." | AJPS 2018;62(3):760-775.
The pair is theoretically motivated: accuracy indexes schema \emph{construction} and success time schema \emph{automation}, the two CLT phases of skill acquisition, so both dimensions of the construct are measured and reported. The confirmatory decision, however, is carried by accuracy alone: a single pre-specified primary variable keeps the confirmatory claim unambiguous and concentrates statistical power where the theoretical prediction is sharpest \parencite{ich1998,pocock1997}. % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [ich1998] sec 2.2.2, p.7: "There should generally be only one primary variable." (EMA Step-5 PDF CPMP/ICH/363/96, read page-by-page; preceding sentence defines the primary variable as the one "capable of providing the most clinically relevant and convincing evidence".) [pocock1997] = Pocock, Geller & Tsiatis, Biometrics 1987 (in power_refs.bib).
The full defence of the outcome pair and of this hierarchy, including the rejected co-primary and composite alternatives, is given in Appendix~\ref{app:coprimary-rationale}.

\paragraph{Primary analysis: accuracy.}
Because each participant is measured at both unassisted waves, the two proportions correct are correlated within person; accuracy is therefore analysed with a participant-level linear mixed model (REML)~\parencite{detry2016}, % [detry2016]: quoted at first citation.
whose random intercept absorbs that dependence and which yields, from a single fit, the immediate-wave arm effects (the primary estimand) together with their change at the delayed wave (the persistence estimand):
\begin{equation}
Y_{iw} \;=\; \beta_0 + \beta_1\,\mathrm{Socratic}_i + \beta_2\,\mathrm{Unrestricted}_i + \delta\,\mathrm{Wave}_w + \beta_4\,(\mathrm{Socratic}_i \times \mathrm{Wave}_w) + \beta_5\,(\mathrm{Unrestricted}_i \times \mathrm{Wave}_w) + \gamma_1\,\mathrm{MiniVLAT}^{c}_i + \gamma_2\,\mathrm{AIUse}^{c}_i + u_i + \varepsilon_{iw},
\label{eq:primary-acc}
\end{equation}
with Google-only the reference category and $\mathrm{Wave}$ a $0/1$ dummy (immediate $=0$, delayed $=1$), so that every arm coefficient is anchored at the immediate post-test. The parameters map onto the hypotheses as follows. $\beta_2$, the immediate-wave Unrestricted--Google difference, adjudicates H1$_a$ ($H_0\colon \beta_2 \ge 0$ against $H_1\colon \beta_2 < 0$). $\beta_1$, the immediate-wave Socratic--Google difference, adjudicates the non-inferiority hypothesis H2$_a$ ($H_0\colon \beta_1 \le -\Delta$ against $H_1\colon \beta_1 > -\Delta$, for the pre-specified margin $\Delta$; see Hypothesis testing). The linear combination $\beta_1 - \beta_2$ adjudicates the supporting Socratic--Unrestricted contrast ($H_0\colon \beta_1 - \beta_2 \le 0$ against $H_1\colon \beta_1 - \beta_2 > 0$). Persistence (H5$_a$) is the claim that the H1$_a$ deficit is still present at 72 hours; it is therefore adjudicated by the \emph{delayed-wave simple effect} $\beta_2 + \beta_5$ ($H_0\colon \beta_2 + \beta_5 \ge 0$ against $H_1\colon \beta_2 + \beta_5 < 0$), while the interaction $\beta_5$ itself estimates how much the deficit grows or shrinks between waves and is reported with its confidence interval rather than tested as the persistence claim. Here $u_i$ is a participant random intercept and superscript $c$ denotes the mean-centred prognostic baseline covariates ($\mathrm{MiniVLAT}$, $\mathrm{AIUse}$), adjusted for precision; single-wave linear models elsewhere in this plan use HC3 robust standard errors. A practical property of this anchoring is that the primary contrasts are measured in the same session as training, before the attrition window opens, so they are essentially complete-data by design. The choice of the mixed model over per-wave analyses and repeated-measures ANOVA, the covariate adjustment, the compound-symmetry restriction and its guards, and the assumption diagnostics are detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:accuracy-model}.

\paragraph{Key secondary analysis: success time.}
Not every item yields a correct answer, so the time to a correct response is right-censored whenever the answer is wrong or ``I do not know''. It is therefore analysed as a censored failure time with a Cox proportional hazards model~\parencite{cox1972}, stratified by wave so that each wave keeps its own baseline hazard, with wave-specific arm effects:
\begin{equation}
\lambda_{w}(t \mid \cdot) \;=\; \lambda_{0w}(t)\,\exp\!\big(\theta_1\,\mathrm{Socratic}_i + \theta_2\,\mathrm{Unrestricted}_i + \theta_3\,(\mathrm{Socratic}_i \times \mathrm{Wave}_w) + \theta_4\,(\mathrm{Unrestricted}_i \times \mathrm{Wave}_w) + \eta_1\,\mathrm{MiniVLAT}^{c}_i + \eta_2\,\mathrm{AIUse}^{c}_i\big),
\label{eq:primary-st}
\end{equation}
where the event is the submission of a correct answer; incorrect, ``I do not know'', and unanswered items are censored at their recorded response time; and $\mathrm{Wave}_w$ is the same $0/1$ dummy as in Equation~\eqref{eq:primary-acc}, its main effect absorbed by the strata. A hazard ratio above one indicates a higher instantaneous rate of producing correct answers---that is, greater fluency. With this coding $\theta_2$ is the immediate-wave log hazard ratio of Unrestricted against Google and adjudicates H1$_b$ ($H_0\colon \theta_2 \ge 0$, i.e.\ $\mathrm{HR} \ge 1$, against $H_1\colon \theta_2 < 0$); the combination $\theta_1 - \theta_2$ adjudicates the supporting Socratic--Unrestricted fluency contrast of H2$_b$ ($H_0\colon \theta_1 - \theta_2 \le 0$); and the delayed-wave combinations $\theta_1 + \theta_3$ and $\theta_2 + \theta_4$ provide the exploratory success-time persistence read (H5$_b$), reported as estimates with confidence intervals only. No non-inferiority claim is made on the time scale. Because items are nested in participants, inference uses participant-clustered robust standard errors. Success time is a key secondary outcome: analysed and reported with the same rigour as accuracy, but it does not gate the confirmatory decision. Estimator validity under clustering, the handling of ties, the proportional-hazards check and its accelerated-failure-time fallback, the censoring cap, and the competing-risks rationale for this estimand are detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:successtime-model}.

\paragraph{Hypothesis testing and multiplicity.}
All confirmatory hypotheses are directional and tested one-sided at $\alpha = 0.05$; two-sided 95\% confidence intervals accompany every estimate. The confirmatory family concerns the primary outcome only and is tested with a fixed-sequence gatekeeping procedure \parencite{maurer1995,bauer1991,dmitrienko2013}, which controls the familywise error rate in the strong sense while testing each hypothesis at the full $\alpha$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{H1$_a$ (superiority).} Reject $H_0\colon \beta_2 \ge 0$ if the one-sided $p < 0.05$.
    \item \textbf{H2$_a$ (non-inferiority).} Tested only if step 1 rejects. Non-inferiority is declared if the lower bound of the two-sided 90\% confidence interval for $\beta_1$ (equivalently, the one-sided 95\% bound) exceeds $-\Delta$ \parencite{schuirmann1987,lakens2017,lakens2018}. The margin is pre-specified at $\Delta = 8.5$ percentage points, defined by 50\% preservation of the $\approx$17-percentage-point reference deficit that anchors H1 \parencite{shen2026}: the Socratic arm must retain at least half of the control's advantage over the harmful comparator. The margin is justified on substantive rather than power grounds \parencite{piaggio2012,ema2005,althunian2017,dagostino2003}; its derivation and the non-inferiority power analysis are given in Appendix~\ref{app:power}. % D6-locked margin; reversible to the stricter SESOI-unified 6.25 pp via one constant in the power script --- see CLAUDE.md D6.
    That same reporting guidance additionally requires the claim to hold in the per-protocol population \parencite{piaggio2012}, and the 90\% interval is reported whatever the verdict \parencite{cumming2014}.
    \item \textbf{Supporting contrast.} Tested only if step 2 passes: reject $H_0\colon \beta_1 - \beta_2 \le 0$ if the one-sided $p < 0.05$ (Socratic superior to Unrestricted---the contrast establishing that the scaffold, not mere LLM presence, protects learning).
\end{enumerate}
Testing stops at the first non-rejection; any hypotheses beyond that point are reported as estimates with confidence intervals, without confirmatory claims. Two further pre-specified secondary hypotheses on the accuracy scale---the Bloom-order interaction (H4$_a$, Equation~\eqref{eq:bloom}) and delayed-wave persistence (H5$_a$, Equation~\eqref{eq:primary-acc})---form a separate family tested one-sided with the Holm step-down procedure at familywise $\alpha = 0.05$ \parencite{holm1979}. % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [holm1979] abstract: "It is shown that the test has a prescribed level of significance protection against error of the first kind for any combination of true hypotheses." | Scand J Statist 1979;6:65-70.
The two directional key-secondary success-time contrasts (H1$_b$ and the H2$_b$ supporting contrast) form a third, Holm-adjusted family interpreted as supporting evidence only; the Socratic--Google hazard ratio, for which no directional hypothesis is specified, accompanies them as an estimate with its confidence interval. The aided-block productivity indicators (H3) are controlled at a false discovery rate as converging, non-confirmatory indicators \parencite{benjamini1995}. All remaining quantities are exploratory and carry no error-rate claim; these include the arm-by-Mini-VLAT moderation of the immediate-wave accuracy deficit motivated in Section~\ref{sec:theory}, fitted by adding arm-by-covariate interaction terms to Equation~\eqref{eq:primary-acc} and reported as an estimate with its confidence interval, without a directional claim or verdict. The choice of the gatekeeper over a flat Holm correction and over graphical $\alpha$-recycling, and the full non-inferiority machinery, are defended in Appendix~\ref{app:multiplicity}.

\paragraph{Secondary analysis I: Bloom-level concentration.}
To test RQ2, the focal analysis contrasts lower-order (Remember, Understand) against higher-order (Analyze, Evaluate) accuracy on the immediate unaided post-test, modelling item-level correctness with a logistic mixed model with crossed participant and item random intercepts \parencite{baayen2008,barr2013}:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{logit}\big(\Pr[Y_{im}=1]\big) \;=\; \beta_0 + \beta_1\,\mathrm{Socratic}_i + \beta_2\,\mathrm{Unrestricted}_i + \beta_3\,\mathrm{Order}_m + \beta_4\,(\mathrm{Socratic}_i \times \mathrm{Order}_m) + \beta_5\,(\mathrm{Unrestricted}_i \times \mathrm{Order}_m) + \gamma_1\,\mathrm{MiniVLAT}^{c}_i + \gamma_2\,\mathrm{AIUse}^{c}_i + u_i + v_m,
\label{eq:bloom}
\end{equation}
where $Y_{im}$ is the correctness of participant $i$ on item $m$, $\mathrm{Order}_m=1$ for higher-order items and $0$ for lower-order,
and $u_i$ and $v_m$ are crossed participant and item random intercepts.
The focal, directional quantity is the arm $\times$ order interaction $\beta_5$ for the Unrestricted--Search contrast---the item-level expression of H4$_a$ ($H_0\colon \beta_5 \ge 0$ against $H_1\colon \beta_5 < 0$ on the log-odds scale): a negative value denotes a deficit larger at the higher-order level. It is tested within the secondary Holm family (see Hypothesis testing and multiplicity), with the confirmatory one-sided test taken from the participant-clustered logistic GEE fit of this mean structure (see Software and reproducibility; Appendix~\ref{app:bloom-analysis}). The success-time analogue H4$_b$ is exploratory, reported as order-specific estimates with confidence intervals only. The preference for this binary partition over a four-level analysis, the supportive four-level trend, the exploratory wave extension and lower-order equivalence test, and the validity check on the level ordering are detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:bloom-analysis}.

\paragraph{Secondary analysis II: productivity.}
The aided practice block measures the productivity side of the trade-off and adjudicates, in supporting fashion, the assisted-performance predictions H3$_a$ (accuracy) and H3$_b$ (success time). Each participant contributes one aided-block value per outcome, so aided accuracy is analysed in the covariate-adjusted single-wave framework
\begin{equation}
Y_i \;=\; \beta_0 + \beta_1\,\mathrm{Socratic}_i + \beta_2\,\mathrm{Unrestricted}_i + \gamma_1\,\mathrm{MiniVLAT}^{c}_i + \gamma_2\,\mathrm{AIUse}^{c}_i + \varepsilon_i,
\label{eq:productivity}
\end{equation}
fitted by ordinary least squares with HC3 standard errors; aided success time uses the Cox model of Equation~\eqref{eq:primary-st} restricted to the training block, without wave strata or wave interactions. In both, $\beta_2$ (respectively the aided-block $\theta_2$) evaluates H3 ($H_0\colon \beta_2 \le 0$ against $H_1\colon \beta_2 > 0$: tool access raises assisted performance). These outcomes are converging indicators rather than confirmatory claims and are controlled at a false discovery rate \parencite{benjamini1995}; they double as the affordance manipulation check. The descriptive productivity--learning frontier plot and the rationale for the rate correct score are detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:productivity}.

\paragraph{Robustness analyses.}
Each test is subjected to multiple robustness tests detailed in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}.

\paragraph{Compliance, contamination, and complier effects.}
The primary analysis retains all randomised participants regardless of compliance, because exclusions based on post-randomisation behaviour can bias the causal estimate \parencite{montgomery2018post}. % [montgomery2018post]: quoted at first citation (Measurement and outcome hierarchy).
Two supplementary analyses characterise the effect of actual tool use: a descriptive per-protocol estimate excluding participants who disclosed external LLM or AI-overview use during training, and a complier average causal effect estimated by two-stage least squares with random assignment as the instrument for compliant tool use, where the instrumented exposure is engagement with the \emph{assigned} tool, so that for each LLM-versus-Google contrast the first stage is the treated arm's engagement rate (a one-sided-noncompliance design, the control arm having no access to the assigned tool) \parencite{angrist1996}, % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [angrist1996] abstract: "the IV estimand is the average causal effect for a subgroup of units, the compliers." | JASA 1996;91(434):444-455.
identified under the standard exclusion and monotonicity assumptions, with compliance rates reported per arm. The per-protocol estimate additionally serves as the pre-specified co-analysis for the non-inferiority hypothesis H2$_a$ (see Hypothesis testing and multiplicity): non-inferiority is claimed only if both the intent-to-treat and per-protocol estimates support it \parencite{piaggio2012}.

\paragraph{Missing data and attrition.}
Attrition is expected primarily at the three-day follow-up. Because the primary contrasts are anchored at the immediate wave, collected in the same session as training, attrition bears chiefly on the persistence estimand (H5$_a$). Differential attrition is assessed by comparing wave-2 return rates across arms and testing whether baseline covariates predict dropout differentially by arm. The mixed model of Equation~\eqref{eq:primary-acc} already uses all available data and is valid under missing-at-random \parencite{detry2016}; % [detry2016]: quoted at first citation (Primary analysis).
as sensitivity, the accuracy and success-time models are re-estimated on multiply imputed data using chained equations with $m = 50$ imputations including arm, baseline covariates, and wave-1 outcomes, pooled by Rubin's rules \parencite{white2011}. % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [white2011] abstract: "Multiple imputation by chained equations is a flexible and practical approach to handling missing data." | Stat Med 2011;30(4):377-399. (bib entry added.)
A pre-registration Monte Carlo validation of this multiple-imputation configuration under missing-at-random recovered an attenuated estimate of the delayed-wave contrast ($-0.060$ against a simulated truth of $-0.080$), whereas the primary mixed model of Equation~\eqref{eq:primary-acc} was essentially unbiased under the same design ($+0.001$ from truth). This plan therefore pre-commits to interpreting any discrepancy between the mixed-model and multiple-imputation estimates in favour of the mixed model, citing this diagnostic as the reason; the multiple-imputation estimate is reported as the pre-specified sensitivity analysis but never supersedes the primary conclusion.
Worst-case sensitivity to attrition that violates missing-at-random is quantified with \textcite{lee2009} bounds on the delayed-wave contrast, % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [lee2009] abstract: "This paper develops an intuitive trimming procedure for bounding average treatment effects in the presence of sample selection." | Rev Econ Stud 2009;76(3):1071-1102. (bib entry added.)
trimming the arm with lower attrition to bound the effect among always-responders without exclusion restrictions. Item-level missingness within completed blocks follows the pre-registered scoring key (unanswered items scored incorrect for accuracy; censored for success time).

\paragraph{Baseline reporting.}
Baseline characteristics are reported descriptively by arm with standardised mean differences; consistent with \textcite{senn1994} % [senn1994]: quoted at first citation (Primary analysis).
and CONSORT \parencite{hopewell2025}, no hypothesis tests of baseline differences are performed.

\paragraph{Manipulation and implementation checks.}
Multiple pre-specified checks verify that the arms instantiated the intended manipulation; following \textcite{montgomery2018post}, % [montgomery2018post]: quoted at first citation (Measurement and outcome hierarchy).
their results inform interpretation and exploratory moderation but never trigger outcome-based exclusion of participants.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Cognitive-load gradient.} Self-reported load is measured with the differentiated cognitive-load questionnaire of \textcite{leppink2013},
    whose ten items resolve into separate intrinsic-, extraneous-, and germane-load subscales rather than the single global index returned by an undifferentiated workload measure such as the NASA-TLX \parencite{hart1988}. % [hart1988]: retained only as the undifferentiated contrast; already in the bibliography.
    The differentiated instrument is required here because the offloading account predicts \emph{component-specific} rather than global differences (Table~\ref{tab:clt-patterns}): the germane subscale, which indexes the schema-construction effort that constitutes learning, is predicted to be depressed in the Unrestricted (outsourcing) arm relative to both the Socratic and the Control arm, whereas the extraneous subscale is predicted to be highest in the search-based Control arm.
    The instrument's three-factor structure was established by confirmatory factor analysis and replicated across content domains and instructional contexts in its development studies \parencite{leppink2013,leppink2014}, and it has since been independently validated, with acceptable subscale reliabilities, in problem-based and technology-mediated learning settings \parencite{hadie2016,andersen2021}. This differentiated approach is established in closely comparable research on AI-assisted learning: \textcite{zhang2026} use the Leppink instrument to link generative-AI dependency to the three load types, and \textcite{stadler2024} contrast an LLM with web search using the sibling scale of \textcite{klepsch2017}. This subscale separation is precisely what renders the per-component predictions of Table~\ref{tab:clt-patterns} testable, which a single workload score cannot. Each subscale is compared across arms in the covariate-adjusted framework, and, for the components for which the theory specifies a monotone ordering of the arms, the contrast is supplemented by a Jonckheere--Terpstra test \parencite{jonckheere1954} % [jonckheere1954]: title carries the claim.
    against the pre-registered ordered alternative. A germane-load gradient in which the Unrestricted arm falls below both comparison arms is the central self-report evidence that the arms elicited different degrees of cognitive offloading.
    \item \textbf{Engagement.} Logged tool interactions (search queries; chat messages sent) are summarised per arm
    \item \textbf{Answer-adoption latency.} For every aided item the latency $\Delta_{ij}$ is the interval between the last tool interaction and the moment the participant logs their answer: for the two LLM arms the timestamp of the final assistant message, and---defining the parallel Google-arm analogue---for the search arm the timestamp of the last results-page interaction (query issued or result clicked). Both endpoints are read from the single shared wall-clock event stream of the interaction logger, so $\Delta_{ij}$ is well defined for all three arms without additional instrumentation.
    Short latencies indicate adoption of the tool's output with little independent processing---the behavioural signature of full outsourcing---whereas longer latencies indicate that the participant engaged with the task before committing an answer. This proxy rests on the established use of response latency as a behavioural index of the cognitive effort expended before responding, on the premise that deeper processing takes longer \parencite{callegaro2009,fazio1990}; a very short latency immediately after task-relevant information is delivered is, in particular, the signature used to detect \emph{speeding}---responses returned too fast to have been processed at more than a minimal level---in self-administered web instruments \parencite{zhang2014}, and, under the offloading account, the behavioural correlate of substituting the tool's product for one's own reasoning \parencite{risko2016,kirschner2026}. We nonetheless treat the measure as suggestive rather than definitive. Response latency is not a transparent readout of a single process: a long latency may reflect deliberation, but equally difficulty, distraction, or disengagement, and inferring the generating process from response time alone is a recognised reverse-inference fallacy \parencite{krajbich2015}. Accordingly the latency contrast is interpreted only as a randomised between-arm comparison---under randomisation, an arm-level difference is attributable to the assigned tool rather than to individual differences in ability or item difficulty---and never as an individual-level measure of how much a given participant thought; it is reported as one of several convergent manipulation checks, weighed only where it agrees with the germane-load gradient and the logged engagement counts above. Because the appropriate cutoff for ``direct adoption'' is not known a priori, the check's primary read is threshold-free: the per-arm $\Delta$ distribution is reported, and log-$\Delta$ is modelled with a linear mixed model with participant random intercepts contrasting Unrestricted against Socratic, the Unrestricted arm predicted to show shorter latencies, consistent with the offloading gradient (Table~\ref{tab:clt-patterns}). A participant-level \emph{direct-adoption rate}---the share of aided items with $\Delta_{ij}$ below a cutoff \emph{locked from the pilot $\Delta$ distribution}---is then reported per arm as a descriptive secondary and carried into the exploratory moderation analysis (adoption rate $\times$ unassisted performance). Items with no tool interaction are excluded and their count reported. % IMPLEMENTATION RESOLVED 2026-07-12: assistant-message and answer-submission events are stamped by one shared wall clock (isoNow) in the interaction logger, so Delta = answer timestamp - final tool-interaction timestamp; the model-generation latency (a monotonic performance.now duration) is NOT used. Timestamps carried by pcp_consolidate.js: response_ts, query_ts, click ts, answer_ts.
    \item \textbf{Socratic fidelity.} For the Socratic arm, the rate of judge-triggered regenerations is reported, and a random [20]\% sample of assistant turns is independently double-coded by two human raters for answer disclosure, with inter-rater reliability quantified by Cohen's $\kappa$ \parencite{cohen1960} % VERIFIED 2026-07-12 [cohen1960] title confirmed: "A coefficient of agreement for nominal scales" | Educ Psychol Meas 1960;20:37-46.
    and a pre-registered adequacy threshold of $\kappa \geq 0.80$; the resulting disclosure rate quantifies residual contamination of the Socratic manipulation.
\end{enumerate}
Internal consistency (KR-20) of each 16-item block is reported descriptively.

\paragraph{Software and reproducibility.}
All analyses are conducted in Python 3.11 with a pinned environment and fixed random seed. Estimation uses \texttt{pandas}/\texttt{numpy} for data handling; \texttt{statsmodels} for linear models with HC3 covariance (\texttt{cov\_type="HC3"}), the linear mixed models of Equations~\eqref{eq:primary-acc} (REML via \texttt{MixedLM}, with a pre-specified optimiser fallback chain \texttt{lbfgs}$\to$\texttt{bfgs}$\to$\texttt{powell}$\to$\texttt{cg} invoked when a near-zero random-intercept variance would otherwise leave the default solver singular), the fractional logit as a binomial GLM with robust quasi-maximum-likelihood covariance, multiple imputation via its MICE implementation, and, for the Bloom interaction of Equation~\eqref{eq:bloom}, a participant-clustered logistic GEE (\texttt{cov\_struct=Exchangeable}) as the confirmatory estimator, with the crossed-random-effects \texttt{BinomialBayesMixedGLM} retained only as corroboration because its variational posterior standard deviations are anticonservative; \texttt{lifelines} for the Cox models with participant-clustered robust covariance (the \textcite{lin1989} sandwich; because \texttt{lifelines}' \texttt{cluster\_col} exposes robust standard errors per coefficient but keeps the naive covariance \emph{matrix}, every arm$\times$wave contrast is formed from the full cluster-robust covariance matrix reconstructed from the clustered score residuals), % [lin1989]: quoted at first citation (Key secondary analysis).
the proportional-hazards diagnostic of \textcite{grambsch1994} % [grambsch1994]: quoted at first citation (Key secondary analysis).
(\texttt{proportional\_hazard\_test}), and the log-normal accelerated failure time fallback; \texttt{scipy}/\texttt{numpy} for the permutation-based randomisation inference and a permutation implementation of the Jonckheere--Terpstra test; and \texttt{pingouin} for Hedges' $g$. Lee bounds are implemented directly from the trimming formulas of \textcite{lee2009}. % [lee2009]: quoted at first citation (Missing data and attrition).
The answer-adoption-latency log-$\Delta$ contrast uses the same participant-random-intercept \texttt{MixedLM}, with a participant-cluster-robust OLS fallback invoked when the random-effect variance is singular; the $\Delta$ values themselves are computed from the shared wall-clock ISO event timestamps carried through the interaction consolidation transform (\texttt{pcp\_consolidate.js}), never from the model-generation latency.
Multiplicity adjustments (Holm; Benjamini--Hochberg) use \texttt{statsmodels.stats.multitest}, and the non-inferiority bound for H2$_a$ is computed directly from the mixed-model contrast.
Analysis code is written against the pre-registration before unblinding of outcome data, and code and de-identified data are archived in accordance with the pre-registration.
